The Truncated Tree

Digital Equipment Corporation and the Route 128 genealogy that wasn't. Founded the same year as Fairchild — but Massachusetts non-compete law meant the chain stopped after two generations.

1957 — DEC founded (same year as Fairchild) 2 generations of spinoffs — all acquired $9.6B — DEC acquired by Compaq, 1998 No successor industry on Route 128 MA common law — non-competes enforced

The symmetry

DEC and Fairchild were both founded in 1957 by engineers who left research institutions to build something better. Both became world-leading technology companies. But Fairchild's departures were legal. DEC's were litigated.

The constraint

Massachusetts common law enforced non-compete agreements under a "reasonableness" standard. DEC sued Data General — its most important spinoff — almost immediately after founding. That litigation signaled to the next generation: leaving carries legal risk.

The non-compete constraint. DEC's engineers were subject to Massachusetts non-compete law from day one. When Edson de Castro left to found Data General — building the 16-bit minicomputer that competed directly with DEC — Olsen sued. The litigation created a chilling effect on departures.
