The Truncated Tree

Digital Equipment Corporation and the Route 128 genealogy that wasn't — how Massachusetts non-compete law constrained the knowledge compounding that built Silicon Valley.
DEC itself Direct spinoffs (Massachusetts) Talent that escaped to California Decline / acquisition Counterfactual (what mobility could have built)
The non-compete constraint. DEC's engineers were subject to Massachusetts non-compete law from day one. When Edson de Castro left to found Data General, DEC sued immediately — a legal battle that chilled further departures. Compare this to California, where Fairchild alumni were legally free to compete. The result: Silicon Valley compounded knowledge across six generations. Route 128 did not.
