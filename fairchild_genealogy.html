The Fairchild Genealogy

Six generations of Silicon Valley — from eight departures in 1957 to a $2 trillion legacy. Click any node to explore the story.

The founding act

On September 18, 1957, eight engineers left Shockley Semiconductor and founded a direct competitor. California's Business & Professions Code §16600 made this legally consequence-free. In Massachusetts, identical circumstances would have triggered litigation over non-compete agreements. The difference shaped everything that followed.

The compounding principle

Each Fairchild departure seeded the next: engineers → companies → VC firms → funded companies → more engineers. By 1986 the SEMI genealogy chart traced 126 semiconductor companies to Fairchild departures. By 2014, 92 of them had gone public, with a combined market value of $2.1 trillion.

The California difference. Every departure on this tree was legally uninhibited by California's §16600. In Massachusetts, each move would have required navigating a non-compete agreement. Different law. Same talent. Radically different outcomes.
