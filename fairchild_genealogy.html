The Fairchild Genealogy

Six generations of Silicon Valley — from eight departures in 1957 to a $2 trillion legacy. Click any node to explore the story.

The founding act

On September 18, 1957, eight engineers left Shockley Semiconductor and founded a direct competitor. California's Business & Professions Code §16600 made this legally consequence-free. In Massachusetts, the same move would have risked non-compete litigation.

The compounding principle

Each Fairchild departure seeded the next: engineers → companies → VC firms → funded companies → more engineers. By 1986 the SEMI genealogy chart traced 126 semiconductor companies to Fairchild. By 2014 descendant market cap exceeded Canada's GDP.

The California difference. Every departure on this tree was legally uninhibited by California's §16600. In Massachusetts, each move would have required navigating a non-compete agreement. The departures that built this $2 trillion lineage were not exceptional courage — they were ordinary ambition, freed by law.
