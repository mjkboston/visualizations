Lotus Development Corporation: The Truncated Arc

Cambridge, MA · 1982–1995. The first "killer app" of the PC era. $1B in revenue. 70% spreadsheet market share. Then: failure to pivot to Windows, IBM acquisition, brand retirement, and an almost complete lack of Massachusetts spinoffs.

1982 — Founded Cambridge, MA $1B peak annual revenue $3.5B IBM acquisition 1995 2013 — 1-2-3 discontinued 0 significant MA spinoffs

The founding

Mitch Kapor left VisiCorp with $1.7M and hired Jonathan Sachs — who had himself left Data General — to write a better spreadsheet. Sachs wrote all of Lotus 1-2-3 in assembly language for the IBM PC.

The failure

Lotus declined Microsoft's repeated invitations to develop for Windows, betting instead on IBM's OS/2. When Windows 3.0 shipped in 1990 and was adopted by millions, Lotus had no competitive Windows spreadsheet.

The Ray Ozzie exception — and what it proves. Lotus's single most consequential alumnus left in 1984. Ray Ozzie founded Iris Associates (which built Lotus Notes, worth ~$3B to IBM) because he was allowed to depart and form a new company.

The Ray Ozzie chain: what compounding looks like when it escapes

1

PLATO → Lotus (1983–84)

Ozzie joins Lotus recruited by Sachs and Kapor to work on Symphony. His visceral understanding of networked computing becomes the seed for later products.

2

Lotus → Iris Associates (December 7, 1984)

Ozzie leaves Lotus with Kapor's explicit blessing — a departure that becomes the crucial branch point in this genealogy.

3

Iris builds Notes (1984–89)

Five years of development. Notes 1.0 ships December 7, 1989 — exactly five years after the Iris founding.

4

Lotus acquires Iris ($84M, 1994) → IBM acquires Lotus ($3.5B, 1995)

Analysts estimate ~$3B of the $3.5B IBM paid was essentially for Notes and the collaboration business it represented.

5

Ozzie leaves IBM/Lotus (1997) → founds Groove Networks

After the IBM integration, Ozzie departs to found Groove — another company in the same Boston orbit.

6

Microsoft acquires Groove (2005) → Ozzie succeeds Gates

Ozzie becomes Microsoft CTO, then Chief Software Architect, where his Lotus-originated ideas eventually inform Azure.
