EMC Corporation: The Terminated Tree

The terminated tree

EMC was founded the same year as many great California companies — and grew to comparable scale. But where Fairchild's engineers freely departed to found 126 traced companies, EMC's engineers were bound by Massachusetts non-compete law and culture. The knowledge that built EMC depreciated inside a single corporate structure rather than compounding across generations.

The acquisition strategy

EMC grew primarily by acquiring California companies — VMware (Stanford alumni), Documentum (CA), Legato (Sun Microsystems alumni). The most valuable assets in EMC's legacy were California-origin knowledge purchased rather than Massachusetts knowledge cultivated. When Dell acquired EMC in 2016, it was largely buying California-built technology.

"Large companies like EMC often rely on the argument that non-competes offer much-needed protection against intellectual property infringement. What doesn't hold is that the EMCs of the world don't appear to have any trouble competing on a national scale, despite the fact that the non-competes they tout so steadfastly are rare outside of Massachusetts."
— Steve Kraus, President, New England Venture Capital Association (NEVCA), testimony to Massachusetts Legislature, 2014
