EMC Corporation: The Terminated Tree

Massachusetts' largest technology company — founded 1979, acquired by Dell 2016 for $67 billion. Technically dominant. Genealogically terminal. Zero significant spinoff companies.

1979 — Founded Hopkinton, MA $67B — Dell acquisition, 2016 0 significant spinoff companies 40,000 peak employees Fairchild comparison: $2.1T in descendants

The terminated tree

EMC was founded the same year as many great California companies — and grew to comparable scale. But where Fairchild's engineers freely departed to found 126 traced companies, EMC's engineers were constrained by Massachusetts non-compete law and organizational culture.

The acquisition strategy

EMC grew primarily by acquiring California companies — VMware (Stanford alumni), Documentum (CA), Legato (Sun Microsystems alumni). The most valuable assets in EMC's legacy were California-born spinoffs.

"Large companies like EMC often rely on the argument that non-competes offer much-needed protection against intellectual property infringement. What doesn't hold is that the EMCs of the world were built on the backs of talent that left competitors to build value elsewhere."
— Steve Kraus, President, New England Venture Capital Association (NEVCA), testimony to Massachusetts Legislature, 2014
