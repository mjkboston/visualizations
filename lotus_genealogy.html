Lotus Development Corporation: The Truncated Arc

Cambridge, MA · 1982–1995. The first "killer app" of the PC era. $1B in revenue. 70% spreadsheet market share. Then: failure to pivot to Windows, IBM acquisition, brand retirement. No successor ecosystem. Click any node to explore.

The founding

Mitch Kapor left VisiCorp with $1.7M and hired Jonathan Sachs — who had himself left Data General — to write a better spreadsheet. Sachs wrote all of Lotus 1-2-3 in assembly language in ten months. The product launched January 26, 1983. In its first year Lotus was a $50M company; in its second, $150M. It was the fastest-growing software company in history.

The failure

Lotus declined Microsoft's repeated invitations to develop for Windows, betting instead on IBM's OS/2. When Windows 3.0 shipped in 1990 and was adopted by millions, Lotus had no competitive product. Excel — built from scratch for Windows by engineers who could move freely across the Seattle and California ecosystems — filled the vacuum. Lotus never recovered its leadership.

The Ray Ozzie exception — and what it proves. Lotus's single most consequential alumnus left in 1984. Ray Ozzie founded Iris Associates (which built Lotus Notes, worth ~$3B of IBM's $3.5B acquisition price), then founded Groove Networks (acquired by Microsoft 2005), then succeeded Bill Gates as Microsoft's Chief Software Architect, then announced Windows Azure. His career is the Fairchild chain in miniature — PLATO → Lotus → Iris → Groove → Microsoft → Azure. But notice: the value compounded outside Massachusetts. California and Seattle captured what Cambridge incubated.

The Ray Ozzie chain: what compounding looks like when it escapes

1

PLATO → Lotus (1983–84)

Ozzie joins Lotus recruited by Sachs and Kapor to work on Symphony. His vision of collaborative networked software — inspired by PLATO — becomes clear. He proposes building a PC-network version.

2

Lotus → Iris Associates (December 7, 1984)

Ozzie leaves Lotus with Kapor's explicit blessing — a deal under which Iris owns development, Lotus owns distribution. Founded in Westford, MA. Core team: Halvorsen, Kawell, Beckhardt — all PLATO alumni.

3

Iris builds Notes (1984–89)

Five years of development. Notes 1.0 ships December 7, 1989 — exactly five years after founding. Integrated email, document database, workflow, bidirectional replication. By 1993: 2,000 companies, 500,000 users. Eventually: 150 million users.

4

Lotus acquires Iris ($84M, 1994) → IBM acquires Lotus ($3.5B, 1995)

Analysts estimate ~$3B of the $3.5B purchase price was attributable to Notes. IBM CEO Lou Gerstner wanted Notes specifically to compete with Microsoft. The product built by someone who left Lotus was worth 6× the value of everything Lotus built internally.

5

Ozzie leaves IBM/Lotus (1997) → founds Groove Networks

After the IBM integration, Ozzie departs to found Groove Networks in Beverly, MA — peer-to-peer secure collaboration for mobile teams. A third vision from the same PLATO-inspired conviction about networked collaboration.

6

Microsoft acquires Groove (2005) → Ozzie succeeds Gates

Ozzie becomes Microsoft CTO, then Chief Software Architect — succeeding Bill Gates. In October 2008 he announces Windows Azure. The tacit knowledge built at Lotus/PLATO/Iris ultimately seeds Microsoft's cloud strategy.
