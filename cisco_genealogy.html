The Cisco Genealogy: 40 Years of California §16600 Compounding

Stanford (1984) → Cisco → Juniper → Aruba → Mist → HPE. Cisco → Palo Alto Networks ($100B+). Cisco → Meraki → Samsara ($20B). Former Cisco CEO John Chambers founds Nile competing with Cisco.

1984 — Cisco founded, Menlo Park CA 4 generations documented $100B+ Palo Alto Networks $20B Samsara — Meraki founders' second company §16600 — every departure legally automatic

The mechanism

Cisco engineers depart freely under California §16600, found competing companies, grow them, and Cisco either acquires them (Sourcefire $2.7B, Meraki $1.2B, Splunk $28B) or competes against them (Palo Alto Networks, Juniper).

The Massachusetts contrast

The Route 128 networking cluster (Proteon, Interlan, Wellfleet, Cabletron, Cascade, Bay Networks, Sycamore, Starent) operated in the same era, in the same industry, with comparable talent. Result: zero surviving descendants.

The CEO who competed with his own company. In 2018, John Chambers — who had run Cisco for 20 years — departed and co-founded Nile, a startup competing directly with Cisco in networking. This was legal, normal, and expected under §16600.

Key chains in the Cisco genealogy

1

Cisco → Juniper → Fungible → Microsoft

Pradeep Sindhu left Xerox PARC to co-found Juniper (1996). Later co-founded Fungible (2015, sold to Microsoft 2023).

2

Cisco → Meraki ($1.2B) → Samsara (NYSE: IOT, $20B)

Biswas and Bicket founded Meraki, sold to Cisco. Then founded Samsara for IoT infrastructure at scale.

3

Cisco → Aruba ($3B) → Mist → Juniper ($405M) → HPE

Melkote (ex-Cisco SVP) founded Aruba, sold to HP. Co-founded Mist AI networking, acquired by Juniper.

4

Cisco → Palo Alto Networks ($100B+)

Nir Zuk (Check Point → NetScreen → Cisco/Juniper context) founded PAN in 2005 — now one of the largest software companies.

5

Cisco CEO → Nile (competing with Cisco)

John Chambers ran Cisco for 20 years, departed 2015, founded Nile 2018 competing with Cisco in enterprise networking.
