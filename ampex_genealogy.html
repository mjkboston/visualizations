Ampex: Silicon Valley's Hidden Root

Founded 1944, Redwood City, CA. One company incubated the founders of Dolby Laboratories, Atari, Oracle, and Pixar — and through them, Apple and Salesforce. Five generations of legally uninhibited departures spanning broadcasting to AI.

1944 — Ampex founded, San Carlos, CA 5 generations of departures Dolby · Atari · Oracle · Pixar — direct spinoffs Apple · Salesforce — second-generation CA §16600 — every departure legally uninhibited

The hidden root

Most histories of Silicon Valley begin with Shockley Semiconductor or Hewlett-Packard. But Ampex — a magnetic tape recording company founded by a Russian émigré in a San Carlos, California wartime factory — incubated a lineage that reaches further: from Bing Crosby's desire to prerecord his radio show to Apple's $3 trillion market cap and Salesforce's $41 billion in annual revenue.

The culture of blessed departure

Ampex didn't just tolerate departures — it blessed them. Management congratulated Nolan Bushnell on his video game prototype and wished him luck. Larry Ellison and Bob Miner left Oracle with "Ampex management's blessing." California §16600 made this legally required; Ampex made it culturally normal. The combination produced five generations of industry-defining companies.

The California principle, one generation before Fairchild. Ampex was founded in 1944 — thirteen years before Fairchild Semiconductor and HP's 1957 IPO. Its departure culture predates Silicon Valley's semiconductor era. When Nolan Bushnell showed his Pong prototype to Ampex's chief engineer, he was congratulated and told it wasn't a business. Under California §16600, that was a business assessment, not a legal prohibition. Bushnell left, founded Atari, hired Steve Jobs, and set in motion the chain that created the personal computer industry. In Massachusetts, the same engineer might have faced a non-compete preventing him from commercializing technology developed at Ampex.

The five chains — Ampex to the present

1

The audio / cinema chain: Ampex → Dolby → every cinema and streaming service

Ray Dolby joined Ampex at 16, built the VTR with a six-person team, left for Cambridge, and founded Dolby Laboratories in 1965. Dolby A, B, Digital, and Atmos became the worldwide audio standard. Every cinema ticket, streaming subscription, and pair of AirPods carries Ampex lineage through Dolby.

2

The gaming / Apple chain: Ampex → Atari → Jobs → Apple → $3 trillion

Nolan Bushnell built Pong at Ampex. Left (with management's blessing), founded Atari, hired Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak. Jobs used Atari to connect to Don Valentine (Sequoia, Fairchild lineage), who connected him to Mike Markkula (Fairchild/Intel), who co-founded Apple. Ampex is two steps from Apple's founding.

3

The enterprise software chain: Ampex → Oracle → Salesforce → SaaS era

Ellison, Miner, and Oates left Ampex in 1977 — with management's blessing — to found Oracle. Marc Benioff joined Oracle at 23, became youngest VP, was mentored by Ellison, and left 13 years later to found Salesforce, pioneering the SaaS model. The $41B Salesforce and the $500B+ SaaS sector trace directly to Ampex's CIA database project.

4

The visual effects / CGI chain: Ampex → Lucasfilm → Pixar → modern cinema

Tom Porter built the world's first commercial digital paint program (AVA) at Ampex. Alvy Ray Smith hired him away to Lucasfilm. Porter and Tom Duff developed "Porter-Duff compositing" — the seminal technique of computer graphics. Porter was one of Pixar's 40 founding employees. Every Pixar film and modern visual effects movie uses techniques developed at Ampex.

5

The §16600 thread: California law at every departure

Every link in every chain — Dolby, Bushnell, Ellison, Porter — was enabled by California Business and Professions Code §16600. In Massachusetts, each departure to found a direct competitor or adjacent company would have required navigating non-compete agreements. The culture of "blessed departure" that Ampex embodied was legally mandated by California and legally impeded in Massachusetts.
